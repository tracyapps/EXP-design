# EXP design marketing collaboration brief

October 16, 2026 • Target campaign December 1 • Product owner tracy apps

## What I would love help with

I've built a native Mac design app called EXP [design], and I'm proud of what it
can do. Getting comfortable telling people about it is the harder part for me.
I'd love help making a small, clear launch plan that shows the work, reaches the
people who would actually use it, and gives them an easy next step.

I'm choosing a self-launch. The app already exists and has shipped as v2.6; the
next version is about launch readiness, verification, privacy-respecting analytics
and an honest Pro offer. I want to test both one-time and subscription pricing
with prospective users before choosing. We don't have established demand or
conversion numbers yet. That's what this campaign should help us learn.

## The product in plain language

EXP [design] is a native macOS design tool for making interfaces and handing them
off with more of their meaning intact. It combines a visual canvas, reusable
components, design-language tools and semantic HTML/CSS and agent-ready handoff.
It also tells you where an import or export has limits.

The guiding idea is simple: the tool should get out of the way. Accessibility
belongs in design decisions, and designers deserve to know what happens to their
work as it moves between tools.

Try the existing app and help content at [expdesign.app](https://expdesign.app).
It requires macOS 26.2 or later. The optional Sanaa assistant works through the
user's compatible agent tooling/account; its provider requirements need to be
explained in any demo. EXP does not include an AI model or paid AI subscription.

## Who we should reach first

- Independent Mac designers who need a practical canvas and reusable components.
- Designers and developers working together on semantic, accessibility-aware handoff.
- Accessibility and design-system practitioners willing to try a real workflow
  and tell us where it holds up or falls short.

People rescuing Adobe XD work are a useful secondary audience. Show that import
with a real permitted file and a clear limits report. We don't need a campaign
claiming EXP replaces every part of Figma or XD.

## Three stories we can show

**Keep the meaning with the design.** Show a small component, its roles/notes and
the resulting handoff package. Make the output concrete enough for a developer
to understand why it is useful.

**A canvas that gives the work room.** Show an everyday editing task, reusable
components and the multi-window workspace. Use real interaction when describing
interaction; label screenshot-based promo edits accurately.

**Help that stays under your control.** Show an actual Sanaa critique or repetitive
formatting task, the user's consent and a reversible change. Describe the real
external agent dependency. Avoid unmeasured time-saved claims.

Working line to test: “A native Mac design tool that keeps meaning in the handoff.”
Keep “the tool should get out of the way” as the broader product idea.

## What is ready to work with

The site already has tutorials, product screenshots, release notes and a public
app download. Landscape and portrait promo compositions exist, including a real
Sanaa before/after example; they still need final preview approval and rendering.
We can start by choosing from these assets instead of commissioning a whole new
content library. Final claims and visible UI must match the version being promoted.

A useful starter pack is one 25-second portrait demo with captions, one landscape
demo, three annotated screenshots, one short founder post and one longer technical
handoff walkthrough. Prepare alt text or an equivalent description for images,
readable captions for clips and a text version of the demonstration.

## The offer and next step

Keep the app useful for free, including its existing editing and exports. Pro
should add a benefit people can explain and want; it should not hold their files
hostage. A candidate to test is reusable batch handoff/export workflows. That is
a proposal, not an available feature.

Research cards will compare $89 once with a year of updates and continued use of
eligible versions against $8/month or $79/year. These are test prices, not public
commitments. A recurring offer needs a recurring benefit. Education and hardship
access should fit the final model without making people disclose personal stories.

Before purchase fulfillment is verified, use “See the proposed Pro offer” and
“Tell me you're interested.” Say clearly that it is not for sale yet. Once the
paid benefit, terms and checkout are ready, switch to one straightforward purchase
CTA. Keep the free download easy to find in either case.

community such as r/macapps or Show HN, and friends' own channels where the story
fits. Verify current community rules before posting. A technical handoff demo may
fit Show HN better than a broad feature montage; choose the channel after reviewing
the asset. A newsletter placement can be a second test after the funnel works.
There are no promised traffic, ranking or sales numbers.

## Small roles people can take

- **Message editor:** one hour to make the founder story and three captions clearer.
- **Social collaborator:** adapt one approved clip and propose a two-week posting calendar.
- **Community connector:** suggest three relevant communities/people and explain why
  the product fits. Check with me before sending pitches or posting on my behalf.
- **Launch buddy:** help me publish, answer early questions and review results that week.

I own product decisions, verification, pricing and approval. A friend helping with
marketing should not need to learn the entire roadmap. We can agree on one bounded
contribution, its deadline and whether it's a favor, barter or paid work before
anyone starts. Nobody needs to commit to being an ongoing marketing department.

## What success looks like

First, can new people install EXP and finish a useful create/import → edit →
export/handoff task without coaching? Then, does the priced Pro benefit interest
them enough to sign up or buy when it is available? Finally, do they return to do
real work, and can I support them at a sustainable pace?

We will report raw counts and actual outcomes: eligible task attempts, confirmed
Pro interest, independent purchases/refunds, returning users where they opt into
measurement, and support time. Views and likes help us understand reach; they
are not proof of product value. Small samples are a reason to listen carefully.

Experiment spend is capped at $500 pending approval. Start with existing assets
and organic distribution. Legal review and payment fees are separate costs.

## Claims we can stand behind

Use “native macOS,” “semantic HTML/CSS handoff,” “accessibility-aware design tools,”
“explicit import/export limits,” and demonstrations that match verified behavior.
Keep “local-first” precise: files/editing are local, while update checks, chosen
imports, optional analytics and agent/provider actions can use the network.

Avoid “ADA compliant,” “fully accessible,” “all imports are lossless,” “everything
stays on your Mac,” invented customer results, or market/rebuild valuations from
the assessment. Any quoted user or customer work needs their explicit permission.
