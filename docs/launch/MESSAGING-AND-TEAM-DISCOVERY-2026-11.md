**Headline:** how does your team actually work?

**Lead:** the component library. the code. the spreadsheet somebody made because the other two don’t agree.

**Body:** i’m exploring a team version of EXP [design]. i have opinions. many of them. but it’s been a while since i worked inside a corporate design team, and i’d rather ask than build around my own outdated assumptions.

**Submit:** send my answers

**Optional follow-up:** up for a follow-up? optional, actually. your answers are already saved.

### Research outline (implementation and rollout in TEAM-RESEARCH-2026-11.md)

1. **What's your role?** Designer / design-system lead / developer or design engineer / team leader / IT, security or procurement / other.
2. **What is the biggest recurring problem in your current design-to-development workflow?** One short optional text box; “nothing significant” is a valid answer. Ask this before showing a feature wishlist.
3. **Where do your team members work?** Mac / Windows / Linux / mixed / not sure. Optional team-size range: 1 / 2–5 / 6–20 / 21–100 / 100+.
4. **Which tools would a new tool need to work with?** Multi-select categories: design tools / coded component library / Storybook or documentation / tokens / repository and delivery tools / other / not sure. Reveal optional names only for selected categories, with free text available.
5. **What's the biggest barrier to trying a new tool?** Migration / team compatibility / IT or security approval / missing capability / cost / time to evaluate / no barrier / not sure. Optional explanation.

Then allow submission without email. On the thank-you screen, offer an optional follow-up email and a separate unchecked choice: “You may contact me about this research and invite me to a conversation.” General product marketing needs its own separate choice. Don't call the survey anonymous until the implementation and host logging support that claim.

Offer a skippable “add technical details” step AFTER the core response is saved. Keep “other” and “not sure” everywhere relevant. Don't send a confirmation email or retain an address without the applicable request/permission. ### Technical detail: separate the layers

React and Angular are UI technologies, not programming languages. Collect language and framework separately, with optional versions when compatibility matters. These are interview prompts, not promised EXP integrations.

| Layer | Examples to offer or probe | What the answer informs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Languages | JavaScript, TypeScript, HTML/CSS, C#, Swift, Kotlin, Dart | What actual output teams consume |
| UI frameworks/platforms | React/Next.js, Angular (distinguish AngularJS), Vue/Nuxt, Svelte, Web Components, .NET/Blazor, React Native, native mobile | Where one bounded integration could help |
| Components | Internal library, Material UI, Angular Material, other vendor library | Existing source of truth; ownership and licensing constraints |
| Docs and component development | Storybook, Chromatic, Zeroheight, Supernova, custom docs | How people inspect examples and approved usage |
| Tokens | Figma Variables, Tokens Studio, Style Dictionary, DTCG JSON, CSS variables, custom pipeline | Format, aliases, modes, naming and transformation needs |
| Source and delivery | GitHub/GitLab/Azure DevOps, package registry, CI | Review, versioning, release and distribution process |
| Quality and planning | axe, Playwright, visual regression, Jira/Linear, manual reviews | How a team validates and adopts changes |
| AI policy | Allowed providers, disabled AI, approval and data-sharing restrictions | Whether optional agent workflows can be evaluated at all |

Ask “which is authoritative when they disagree?” and “who can change it?” Tool names alone don't explain the workflow. Storybook documents multiple frameworks and varying feature coverage; don't treat “uses Storybook” as proof of one universal integration. DTCG has a stable 2025.10 specification, but that does not establish a particular tool's conformance or EXP support.

### Follow-up interview: 20 minutes

- “Walk me through the last time the design library and coded library disagreed.” What happened, who fixed it, and what was delayed? Ask for a recent example before showing EXP.
- “What have you already tried?” Find the current workaround, its effort and its owner.
- “Where could a second tool fit without replacing the whole system?” Test a complementary workflow as well as replacement interest.
- “What would you need to see in a small pilot to call it successful?” Agree on an observable task, output and acceptable limits.
- “Who uses it, who approves it, and who controls the budget?” These may be three different people.
- “What happened the last time you approved a new design/developer tool?” Learn actual purchasing steps, timeline, contract expectations and pricing unit.
- Only after understanding the problem, ask about a concrete proposed paid benefit and budget range. An email signup or hypothetical “sounds good” is not willingness to pay.

Recruit across small agencies, in-house product teams and design-system teams, including a few IT/security approvers. A starting target is six interviews across at least three organizations—not statistical validation. Avoid drawing the whole roadmap from friendly users or the biggest company's longest checklist.

### Approval discovery: ask requirements, don't assume them

Ask respondents to distinguish **required before a pilot**, **required before rollout**, **preferred**, and **not applicable**. Establish whether they are evaluating a local desktop editor, an optional agent connection, or a future hosted service; the requirements may differ.

- Identity/admin: SSO, SCIM, offboarding, roles, audit history.
- Deployment: allowed macOS versions, managed installation/update policy, offline/network rules, device management.
- Information handling: data categories, storage/processing locations, encryption expectations, retention, deletion, backups, subprocessors and AI-provider handling.
- Review evidence: security questionnaire, penetration-test summary, vulnerability response, SOC 2 report or ISO certification if their policy requires it, accessibility conformance report if requested.
- Commercial continuity: billing entity, purchase order/invoice support, support expectations, service commitments, vendor continuity and usable exports if they leave.

These are topics to investigate, not legal advice, a universal compliance checklist, or current EXP capabilities. Do not promise an audit or certification to secure an interview. Avoid collecting confidential architecture diagrams or internal security policies in the public form; let an authorized approver summarize requirements first.

Before collecting responses, settle the actual hosting, access, privacy notice, retention/deletion process and contact purpose. Explain what happens after submission. A proposed follow-up cadence: one personal research invitation to people who opted in; one polite reminder if appropriate; stop when declined. Keep newsletter permission separate.

## A bounded four-week experiment

Suggested effort cap: about 90 minutes/week for distribution and review, plus interviews you explicitly book. No paid spend needed for this first test. These are planning targets, not promised conversion rates.

| Week | Work | Evidence to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Make one demo; publish one LinkedIn post; invite 2–3 relevant people personally | Can people explain the value back? Can a suitable tester finish the task? |
| 2 | Adapt for one permitted community; have two research conversations | Specific objections, workflow examples, installation barriers |
| 3 | Pitch two meetup organizers; publish one useful demo follow-up; continue interviews | Organizer interest and repeated needs across companies |
| 4 | Review outcomes; choose one audience/message to repeat | Completed tasks, requests to use again, interview/pilot commitments |

Community participation gets two 15-minute windows per week. Look for a few specific problems: lost handoff intent, editable XD files, component semantics. Answer only when you can contribute. Stop or change the experiment if four weeks brings no relevant conversations or task attempts; don't compensate by spamming more places.

Log manually: channel/post, angle, qualified replies, volunteered trial outcomes, research opt-ins, conversations, and next action. Do not infer installation or activation from a download link click. Use existing consent-respecting measurement only; add no tracking pixels for this experiment.

A qualified early tester has a compatible Mac, a relevant task and willingness to try it. A team opportunity has a recurring problem, an identifiable owner and a concrete next step. Likes can show reach; they cannot establish either.

A useful decision signal for further Pro work would be several independent organizations describing the same costly problem and at least two willing to scope a pilot. Treat that as a practical heuristic, not a market-size estimate or automatic development commitment.
