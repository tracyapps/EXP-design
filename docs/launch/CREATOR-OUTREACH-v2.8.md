# Creator outreach options — EXP [design]

Drafts for the owner and marketing collaborators. Nothing sent or published.

## When to start

Start with two or three designers for a private, honest tryout now. The existing
Learn page is enough to support a bounded first session; their questions will
show which tutorial to make next. Hold a larger public review push until a new
person can install, make/edit a small piece, save/reopen and export without you
walking them through it. That is a useful readiness check, not a tutorial quota.

Give a tester a short route: https://expdesign.app/download for installation and
requirements, https://expdesign.app/learn for current recordings, a sample file
with permission to share, and a clear account/ticket route for questions.
Mac requirements and limitations should be in the invitation, not a surprise
halfway through setup. Optional Sanaa needs the supported connected local agent;
the editor and exports work without AI or an account.

## A quick “what it is”

EXP [design] is a native Mac design app built around making the work and handing
it off. It has a fast canvas, reusable components, editable imports, a shared
paint system, and exports/handoff that keep design intent useful beyond a picture.
The editor and shipped exports are free. A free account is a separate place for
news, preferences, feedback and support as those tools open up.

It isn’t a cloud whiteboard, multiplayer workspace or prototyping platform. It
isn’t trying to match every Figma menu item. Imported formats have supported
subsets and fidelity reports; I’d rather state a limit than pretend a conversion
was perfect. Pro is an interest conversation today, not a checkout or promised
set of paid features.

## Keep the invitation honest

Replace [specific video/workflow] with an actual relevant example. Do not imply a
paid sponsorship, free perk, priority support, commission or reward unless it has
been separately agreed. Any material connection or later incentive needs an
appropriate disclosure; do not condition a benefit on positive feedback.
[FTC endorsement guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)
explains why disclosures and honest opinions matter. A creator’s approval to test
is not permission to quote them as a testimonial; use a separate explicit choice.
