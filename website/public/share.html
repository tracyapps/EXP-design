A quick guide to talking about EXP
Say thisNot thisBecause…
“Accessibility tools help you find issues and hand off clearer intent.”“Guaranteed ADA-compliant code. Automatically.”A tool can't promise that. Contrast checks, semantic details and critique help, but people still need to build and test the finished experience, including with assistive technology. WCAG is a technical standard; a tool's output is not a legal guarantee.
“Sanaa helps review your design and explains its findings.”“AI catches every accessibility or usability issue.”Critique is useful input. It can miss things or get them wrong. Your judgment and real user testing still matter.
“The editor and shipped exports are free. No account required to try the app.”“Every future feature will be free forever.”The current editor is free. Pro is being explored separately. Don't invent prices, launch dates or promises about what will be included.
“A native macOS design app.”“Works on Windows, Linux and in your browser.”The website is in your browser. The editor runs on a Mac. Check the download page for current system requirements.
“Editable XD/Figma import and flexible handoff.”“Perfect Figma replacement. Every file imports exactly.”Import formats have limits. EXP follows its own UX workflow, and handoff still needs implementation and review.
“Optional web accounts and community profiles.”“Your designs sync across devices, with live multiplayer.”A web account isn't document sync or multiplayer. Planned work isn't a shipped feature.

If you're unsure, link to the current feature descriptions or download page instead of guessing. Please don't imply a partnership, certification or endorsement that hasn't happened.

If you're paid, gifted something or have another connection that matters to your recommendation, be clear about it. And please skip the spam. A useful recommendation to the right person goes a lot further.
